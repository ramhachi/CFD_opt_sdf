# SCREEN-01 (#64) registration draft — decision memo (2026-10-30)

Companion to `docs/screen01_registration_2026_10_30.md` (revision 0, `DRAFT_FOR_PLAN_REVIEW`). Each hard decision: the alternatives considered, the choice, the reason, and the residual risk. Numbers quoted from the registered records are cited there; nothing here is a new measurement. No force of any SCREEN state exists.

## D1. Objective and constraint
**Alternatives.** (a) Maximise the paired flow_32 downforce gain `I = Delta_bar_DF / J_b,48` subject to paired same-phase drag **non-increase** (allowance 0). (b) The same objective with a drag **budget** `Delta_drag <= c_D x drag_parent` (c_D e.g. 3-5 %). (c) An **efficiency** objective (L/D or `Delta_DF - lambda Delta_drag`). (d) Downforce alone, drag report-only (the old Stage S contract).
**Choice: (a)**, with three precise rules: the optimizer (response surface) targets `Delta_drag_pred <= -m_D`, `m_D = 1.0e-3 N`; the measured feasibility filter at selection is the point sign `Delta_bar_drag <= 0`; the sealed decision is the point sign again with a four-way resolution label (`PASS_RESOLVED / PASS_UNRESOLVED / VIOLATION_UNRESOLVED / VIOLATION_RESOLVED`), both violation forms blocking `GAIN`.
**Reasons.** (a) is the project's registered precedent (`cfd_sdf.lowdim03_contract.DRAG_ALLOWANCE_N = 0.0`, "drag_change_at_most_n": 0, used by LOWDIM-03/04 and OPT-01b), the preparation note's proposal, and the form XV-01 re-evaluates on OpenFOAM. It keeps the objective identical to the registered g_min mapping (a downforce gain normalised by `J_b,48`; FID §8), which (c) would not. (b) needs a budget value with no registered source: any c_D large enough to admit pitch makes the answer "pitch +3.1" (M2: +15.4 % downforce for +10.8 % drag, already measured on both solvers), i.e. the L1 test would be trivial and would not exercise the procedure; the visible FID/M2 values show every downforce-gaining single mode costs drag (ratio `Delta_DF / Delta_drag` 1.25 for reflex, 1.4-1.5 for pitch, 2.3 for C1, 2.9 for twist, 6.5 for camber -19.7), so under (a) the problem is a genuine trade (camber - and twist + buy downforce cheaply; pitch - or reflex + sell downforce to recover drag at ratio about 1.4) and the response surface has something to find. (d) contradicts R1/R5 and #64.
**Why the point sign and not a resolved non-increase for the decision.** A resolved non-increase (`Delta_bar_drag + t SE <= 0`) at P = 4 with sigma_d,drag about 7e-4 N demands a measured drag *decrease* of about 8e-4 N (0.3 % of parent drag); an optimizer places its finalist on the constraint boundary, where that rule rejects it about 95 % of the time by construction. The OC (`oc.json`) shows the registered combination (target -m_D, point-sign filter and decision) passes a true 1.2 g_min finalist 93 % of the time and still never passes the null; the `-m_D` filter on the measured values halves the probability of selecting the true best. The resolution label is kept as a registered secondary result, and XV-01 decides feasibility on OpenFOAM with its own, much smaller, drag SE (FID V1 drag SE about 1e-4 N).
**Drag estimator (exact).** `Delta_bar_drag = mean over the stage's common phases of (drag_candidate,k - drag_parent,k)` within the same kernel; `sigma_d` with P - 1 dof; `SE_eff = max(sigma_d, 1.36408347135453e-4 N) / sqrt(P)`; `t = t_{0.95, P-1}`. Flow_48 drag is reported (sign label) and not decisive, because flow_48 has only P = 2 and its drag sigma_d is larger (M2: 2.3e-3 N for pitch 3.1 on flow_32 already).
**Residual risk.** A finalist with a true small drag increase (about +3e-4 N, 0.1 %) passes the sealed point rule about 20 % of the time; it would be caught at XV (OpenFOAM drag SE 1e-4). The m_D value is a planning constant (2 SE of a two-phase drag difference at the FID tier-A drag sigma_d); it only steers the response-surface proposals and is not a decision threshold.

## D2. Search space
**Alternatives.** Heave free (6 modes); heave fixed at 0 (5 modes); dropping pitch for its drag cost; amplitudes beyond A* (29.3 mm camber, 41.1 mm reflex, 2.3 deg twist, which are gate-clean on one sign only).
**Choice.** Five modes pitch, camber, reflex, twist, sweep with heave = 0; box `|u_m| <= 1`, `a_m = u_m A*_m` with the MODE-01 A* (3.1 deg, 19.7 mm, 29.3 mm, 1.1 deg, 11.3 mm); pitch kept.
**Reasons.** Heave: pure heave failed FID tier B (retention 0.08, opposite sign at +31.1), its WaterLily response is grid-dependent (0.62-1.49 between flow_32 and flow_48) because it moves the body against the z lattice that is never phased, and the ride height is a vehicle setting rather than a wing-shape variable; fixing it removes an unsampled systematic from every candidate. This is a SCREEN decision, not an FID rule (the handoff is explicit). Pitch stays because the constraint, not the mode list, should decide its use (negative pitch is a drag reducer the surface can trade), and because #64 and the preparation note name the five-mode space. A* box edges are the only MODE-01-tested amplitudes with both signs gate-clean; the one-sign points would make the box asymmetric and are not needed. Interior and joint points are gated individually (D3).
**Residual risk.** Twist and sweep are weak levers (U1: +1.6e-3 N and about 1e-4 N at A*); they will rank last in stage 1 and act only through the random control and the axial states. The box is small in twist (1.1 deg), set by R0 failures at 2.3 deg; nothing is done about it (R0 kept, D3).

## D3. Finite R0-clean pool
**Alternatives.** (a) A fixed lattice of ladder points (3- or 5-level factorials of the MODE-01 ladders; 3^5 = 243 or 5^3 = 125 per subset). (b) Scrambled low-discrepancy (Sobol) points in the normalised box with a fixed seed, per 3-mode subset plus one 5-D pool for the random control. (c) One 5-D pool for everything, projecting points onto subsets.
**Choice: (b).** 64 points per subset (seed 20261030 + subset index), deterministic extension by 64 while a subset has fewer than 12 admissible members (to 256); the random pool with seed 20261031, consumed in sequence order until 22 admissible members (cap 512 points). Admissible = the unchanged gate stack (13 GEOM-02 v2 gates incl. R0, P22-v2, 10 % smoothed-volume band, clearance 0.15 m) and the X1 export gate; decided for every member before freeze 1 by `scripts/screen01/screen01_pool.py` (which calls `fid01_designs.work` and `_x1` unchanged).
**Reasons.** The ladder lattice aliases with the lattice phases by construction (MODE-01 chose incommensurate ladders for single modes, but products of ladder points are not incommensurate) and gives R0 a structured, not random, pass pattern; Sobol points spread the R0 lottery evenly over the box and the scramble seed makes the pool reproducible. (c) fails because projecting a 5-D point onto a subset changes the geometry and needs a new gate evaluation anyway. Per-subset pools let every one of the 10 possible stage-1 outcomes have a frozen design before any force exists, so the top-mode rule can be applied blind. R0 is kept because GEOM-03 returned NO_GO for the R3 composite and the roadmap selected the R0-clean path; the bias (the finalist is the best admissible member, admissibility is a placement lottery, shear modes and combinations fail more often) is written into the registration §3.6 and the P0 record.
**Exploratory evidence (geometry only, 2026-10-30):** subset {pitch, camber, reflex}: 27 of 64 admissible (42 %, every failure R0), design rank 10 / cond 7.5 (no-intercept rank 9 / cond 4.2), trust region 45 % of the box with 23/27 survivors inside; random 5-D prefix: 3 of 16 admissible (19 %), about 120 points projected for 22; details in `pool_prescreen/README.md`, copied into registration §3.4. They were obtained on the subset the visible data predict ({pitch, camber, reflex}) and a prefix of the random pool; the full P0 for all 10 subsets runs before freeze 1 (about 125-265 s per member on 4 workers; 10 x 64 members is a few CPU hours).
**Residual risk.** If a subset's admissible fraction is low (joint combinations failed R0 in 24 of 30 FID candidates), its design is built from few survivors and its trust region is small; the extension rule bounds this at 256 points, after which the subset is still frozen with whatever it has (and the design admissibility rule decides whether it can carry a response surface at all — an inadmissible subset would have to be reported and the registration not frozen until the pool rule is revised, before any force). Admissibility at the exploration and sealed phases is not re-evaluated (as FID).

## D4. Screening and response-surface design
**Alternatives.** (a) Two stages: single-mode ±A* screen (10 states) → full quadratic on the top 3 modes (9 added points) → at most 3 response-surface proposals → finalist by a fixed rule. (b) One 5-D quadratic (21 coefficients, about 30 points) without a screen. (c) A continuous argmax of the fitted surface built as a new geometry.
**Choice: (a)**, with: top-3 by `max(|Delta_bar_DF(+A*)|, |Delta_bar_DF(-A*)|)` (ties in registered mode order); 9 design points per subset pre-chosen by greedy Bayesian D-optimality from the admissible members (frozen for all 10 subsets); the no-intercept quadratic (differences vanish at the parent) fitted by OLS on 15 points; proposals = the top 3 admissible, unevaluated pool members inside the convex hull of the design points by predicted downforce among those with predicted drag <= -m_D; finalist = the measured-feasible candidate with the largest exploration downforce difference over all 22 evaluated optimizer states; rank insufficiency after freeze (data loss only) → `RS_NOT_FIT`, no proposals, finalist from the evaluated states, nothing repaired.
**Reasons.** (a) is R5 and the #64 text; it is cheaper than (b) (22 vs about 31 states) and the 3-D pool has a usable admissible fraction where a 5-D one would be sparse. The top-3 rule by |effect| is the simplest fixed rule and selects the modes with leverage in either direction, which the constrained trade needs (a drag reducer is as useful as a downforce lever). (c) is excluded because a new continuous point would meet the R0 lottery (about 50-80 % rejection) after the fact, so the finalist must be a pre-gated pool member; restricting proposals to the hull removes extrapolation. Making the finalist the *measured* argmax (and not the surface's prediction) keeps the surface in the role of a proposal generator, so a poor fit cannot select anything unmeasured.
**Disclosure.** The stage-1 ranking is predictable from visible values (pitch > reflex > camber > twist > sweep); stage 1 is kept because its states are the axial points of the surface and the common-phase re-measurement under SCREEN rules, and because the rule has to be applied, not assumed.
**Residual risk.** With about 15-20 admissible members per subset, 9 are used by the design and the proposals choose among the rest inside the hull: the "optimization" is a ranking of a small finite set by a quadratic model. That is what a finite R0-clean pool permits; the control comparison (D7) tells whether the structure beat random sampling at equal budget.

## D5. Phases and grids
**Alternatives.** Exploration P = 1, 2 or 4; sealed P = 4 or 8; flow_48 P = 1, 2 or 4; reusing exploration phases in the decision.
**Choice.** Exploration k = 11, 12 (P = 2) common to G1-G3; sealed flow_32 k = 13-16 (P = 4) and flow_48 k = 13, 14 (P = 2), disjoint from every used phase (0-10 and 11-12); the decision uses the sealed set only; z is never phased.
**Reasons.** UNC-01: `phases_needed = ceil(9 r^2) = 1` for the wide arms (sigma_d 2-5e-4 N against differences of 1e-2 N); P = 2 gives one phase of redundancy and a 1-dof sigma_d for diagnostics; the selection SE (1-3e-4 N) is far below the spacing of candidate gains, so more exploration phases buy nothing for ranking. P = 4 sealed spends about `t_{0.95,3} SE_eff` = 3-4e-4 N (0.1-0.15 % of J_b,48) on resolution; P = 8 would spend 0.09 % and cost 2 GPU h more. flow_48 P = 2 is the roadmap's number and matches the same-phase ratio to the two first sealed flow_32 phases (FID N10). Fresh phases make the decision estimate free of the selection's placement-noise bias (OC: sealed bias about 0, selection bias 1-3e-4 N).
**z consequence.** Heave is fixed; a candidate's different z occupancy is a systematic the phases do not average; XV's 3-axis shifts sample it partly; stated in §4.1.

## D6. Selection bias (optimizer's curse)
**Alternatives.** (a) Raise the GAIN target above g_min (e.g. 3.3-4 %). (b) Shrink the finalist's estimate and decide on the shrunk value. (c) Decide on a sealed fresh-phase re-estimate at g_min and disclose a shrinkage/margin statement for XV.
**Choice: (c).** Decision estimator = the sealed estimate (removes the within-WaterLily placement-noise bias). Disclosure: K_opt, `sigma_disc = 1.4474e-3 N` (FID G3-set residual SD, an upper bound), `E[max_22] = 1.9097`, `b_22 = 2.76e-3 N = 1.04 % of J_b,48` (0.50 % with the retention-spread estimate 7e-4 N), `g_XV,exp = 0.57 (I_s - b_K / J)` against the XV floor 1.2675 % → `XV_MARGIN_ADEQUATE` for `I_s >= 3.26 %`, else `THIN`.
**Reasons.** #64 and the FID handoff forbid adding floors on top of the registered g_min mapping; (a) is a new unregistered threshold. (b) would make the decision depend on a bias model whose sigma is an upper bound. The within-WaterLily bias is small (OC: 1-3e-4 N, 0.1 %) and removed exactly by fresh phases; the WaterLily-to-OpenFOAM bias cannot be measured inside SCREEN and belongs to XV, which is the holdout; the label gives XV the number it needs (the review's §5.4 margin 0.3-1.1 %) without changing L1's definition.
**Residual risk.** A `GAIN` with `XV_MARGIN_THIN` is expected to fail XV with substantial probability (roughly: `g_XV,exp` below 1.27 %); the registration pre-commits XV to proceed anyway, because that case is exactly the one that measures the curse; the cost (about 60-130 CPU h) is XV's registration decision to state.

## D7. Controls
**Alternatives.** Manual: the parent; the best FID single design (reflex -29.3, +1.57e-2 N but +1.25e-2 N drag; camber -19.7, +1.30e-2 N with +2.0e-3 N drag, already an axial state); the FID C1 combination. Random: fewer states (e.g. 10); random directions in phi space.
**Choice.** Manual = FID C1 (camber -11.3 + reflex -11.3; +1.326e-2 N, drag +5.8e-3 N, visible), run at every exploration and sealed flow_32 phase; random = 22 admissible scrambled-Sobol points in the 5-D box, same gates, same phases, its best (same rule) gets the same sealed flow_32 and flow_48 confirmation; NC = the x-translation roll judged against M2.
**Compared on:** the sealed per-phase differences finalist minus control (`OPT_BEATS_RANDOM / RANDOM_BEATS_OPT / UNRESOLVED`, same for the manual control), whether random sampling alone met the L1 rule (`RANDOM_FEASIBLE_GAIN`), and the exploration-phase versions (equal K, so equal selection bias). Labels only; the outcome does not depend on them.
**Reasons.** The parent is the baseline of every difference and cannot be a control; camber -19.7 is already an axial state; C1 is the only visible, gate-clean, human-designed two-mode combination, which is what "a hand-made reference shape" means here. Equal budget (44 runs each side) is the only fair comparison of a sequential procedure against non-adaptive sampling; phi-space random directions are excluded because they are not analytic plates and would meet R0 at 95 % rejection (WIDTH-RULE).
**Residual risk.** The manual control's drag (+5.8e-3 N) makes it infeasible under D1, so `OPT_VS_MANUAL` compares downforce only and is reported as such.

## D8. Budget and stopping
**Choice.** Nine formal kernels (two G1 at about 4.1 h each, G2 2.4 h, G3 1.0 h, two sealed flow_32 at 1.0 h, two flow_48 at 2.0 h) + two smokes: about 18.5 GPU h; cap 24 GPU h of formal session wall governing only the flow_48 push (else `NOT_MEASURED` → `INCOMPLETE`); one identical INFRA_LOSS rerun per kernel; queue > 24 h or quota block > 7 days stops the pushes and is reported.
**Reasons.** Within the 20-25 GPU h envelope and the 30 h weekly quota (sequential generations spread it over weeks anyway); parent-first and pairs-in-kernel as FID; one phase per G1 kernel so a double loss costs one phase, not the stage.
**Residual risk.** The sequential structure has four Kaggle round trips plus two smokes; latency, not GPU hours, is the cost (no deadline, per the user).

## D9. Outcome classes and consequences
**Choice.** `GAIN` (G PASS, D PASS_*, R3 CONFIRMED; sealed data) → XV-01 with the frozen finalist; `NO_GAIN` (no feasible candidate, below minimum at selection, G FAIL, drag violation resolved or unresolved, R3 contradicted, finalist inadmissible) → M4, no extra round; `INCONCLUSIVE` (G unresolved or R3 unresolved) → at most one re-registration; `INCOMPLETE` (data, budget); `INVALID` (NC). Evaluation order in §5.9.
**Reasons.** Fail-closed as FID: only resolved favourable rules give GAIN; a point estimate below the minimum is a negative result, an unresolved one is inconclusive, and an unresolved drag *violation* is treated as a violation because the constraint is an inequality the design must satisfy, not a claim to be resolved. Nothing is repaired from a force.

## D10. XV-01 handoff
**Choice.** `xv_handoff.json` (registration §11) by content hash: parent/finalist NPZ and phi hashes, spec, direction raw and step, lattice and masks, the full gate rows and margins, X1/STL records with the chain check, profile/policy hashes, grids, campaigns/freezes/outputs/tables, phase sets and the z statement, the selection rule and record (K, candidates, surface, proposals), sealed estimates with labels, the control results, the selection-bias statement, analysis and registration hashes. XV re-evaluates every constraint; nothing is inherited as passed.

## D11. Operating characteristics
**Choice.** `scripts/screen01/screen01_oc.py` (numpy/scipy, 20,000 replicates per scenario, 21 scenarios x 2 selection filters); results in registration §5.10. Headline: P(GAIN) = 0 under the null and at 0.5 g_min (no false GAIN through selection alone in 60,000 null replicates); about 1 % at exactly g_min (the lower-bound rule; 16-50 % under a point rule); 93 % at 1.2 g_min and 99.7 % at 1.5 g_min when the finalist's true drag sits at the surface's target -m_D; 33 % / 72 % when it sits on the constraint boundary (lost through D about half the time, the reason for the -m_D target).
**Not simulated.** The response-surface fit, the pool restriction and the top-mode step; the model is about the decision rules on K = 22 candidates.
